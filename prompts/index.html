ENTRY 01.01 · FREE Strategy

Turn a goal into an executable framework

Best for: turning an idea into a sequence you can actually follow.

You are a systems strategist. Turn my goal into a practical operating framework.

GOAL: [specific result]
CURRENT SITUATION: [where I am now]
DEADLINE: [date or timeframe]
AVAILABLE TIME: [hours per week]
BUDGET: [amount]
TOOLS OR SKILLS I ALREADY HAVE: [list]
NON-NEGOTIABLE CONSTRAINTS: [list]

Build:
1. A one-sentence strategy.
2. The 4-7 stages required to reach the goal.
3. The exact output of each stage.
4. The tools, people, and information required.
5. The first three actions I should complete within 48 hours.
6. A weekly scorecard with leading and lagging indicators.
7. The three most likely failure points and prevention measures.

Do not give motivational advice. Flag assumptions and ask no more than three questions only if missing information would materially change the plan.
ENTRY 02.01 · FREE Validation

Pressure-test a business idea

Best for: testing demand before spending heavily.

Act as a skeptical product strategist. Pressure-test this business idea before I invest significant time or money.

IDEA: [describe it]
TARGET CUSTOMER: [who pays]
PROBLEM SOLVED: [problem]
PROPOSED PRICE: [price]
HOW CUSTOMERS FIND IT: [channel]
MY ADVANTAGE: [experience, audience, access, or assets]

Evaluate:
- Urgency and frequency of the problem.
- Whether the customer already pays to solve it.
- Direct and indirect alternatives.
- Trust, legal, technical, or delivery barriers.
- Likely acquisition cost versus customer value.
- What must be true for this to work.

Then create a seven-day validation test costing no more than [budget]. Include the offer, outreach message, target sample size, success threshold, failure threshold, and what I should change based on each possible result. Separate verified facts from assumptions.
ENTRY 07.01 · FREE AI Stack

Choose the right AI tool stack

Best for: comparing tools by workflow rather than hype.

Design the smallest reliable AI tool stack for this workflow:

OUTCOME: [desired result]
USERS: [solo, team size, or customer type]
INPUTS: [documents, calls, leads, images, data, etc.]
REQUIRED OUTPUTS: [deliverables]
CURRENT TOOLS: [list]
MONTHLY BUDGET: [amount]
SECURITY OR PRIVACY NEEDS: [requirements]
TECHNICAL LEVEL: [beginner/intermediate/advanced]

For each workflow stage, recommend:
1. One primary tool and one fallback.
2. Why it fits this exact use case.
3. Current pricing or a note that pricing must be verified.
4. What data moves into and out of it.
5. Setup difficulty and maintenance burden.
6. What should remain under human review.

End with a text-based workflow map, total estimated monthly cost, a setup order, and tools I should avoid because they duplicate functions.
ENTRY 03.01 · FREE Offer

Build a service people understand

Best for: packaging expertise into a clear paid offer.

Act as a productized-service designer. Turn my capability into an offer that a buyer can understand in under 30 seconds.

MY CAPABILITY: [what I can do]
TARGET BUYER: [specific buyer]
EXPENSIVE OR FRUSTRATING PROBLEM: [problem]
RESULT I CAN DELIVER: [result]
DELIVERY TIME: [timeframe]
PROOF OR RELEVANT EXPERIENCE: [proof]
PREFERRED PRICE RANGE: [range]

Create:
- A plain-language offer name.
- One outcome-focused promise without guarantees.
- Exact scope, deliverables, timeline, and client responsibilities.
- Three pricing tiers with meaningful differences.
- Qualification and disqualification criteria.
- A risk-reversal method that does not promise results outside my control.
- A short landing-page section and a five-sentence outreach message.

Remove vague words such as optimize, transform, unlock, and leverage unless followed by a measurable explanation.
ENTRY 06.01 · FREE Operations

Turn messy notes into an SOP

Best for: documenting repeatable work without rewriting everything.

You are an operations analyst. Convert my raw notes into a controlled standard operating procedure.

PROCESS NAME: [name]
RAW NOTES OR TRANSCRIPT:
[paste notes]

Create an SOP containing:
- Purpose and scope.
- Owner, participants, and required approvals.
- Inputs, prerequisites, systems, and access.
- Numbered procedure with one action per step.
- Decision points written as if/then rules.
- Quality-control checks and acceptance criteria.
- Exceptions, escalation path, and records to retain.
- Version number, effective date, and review frequency.

Do not invent missing facts. Put uncertainties in a clearly labeled Open Items table with the person who should resolve each item.
ENTRY 08.01 · FREE Project Control

Create an executive project brief

Best for: turning scattered updates into a decision-ready status report.

Act as a senior project controls lead. Turn the information below into a concise, decision-ready weekly brief.

PROJECT: [name]
REPORTING PERIOD: [dates]
BASELINE MILESTONES: [list]
CURRENT UPDATES, NOTES, AND METRICS:
[paste information]

Return:
1. Overall status: Green, Amber, or Red, with a one-sentence basis.
2. Accomplished this period.
3. Planned next period.
4. Milestone table: baseline date, current forecast, variance, reason.
5. Top risks and issues: owner, impact, probability, mitigation, due date.
6. Decisions needed: decision, decision-maker, deadline, consequence of delay.
7. Budget or resource exceptions.
8. Missing information and contradictions.

Do not hide uncertainty. Distinguish facts, forecasts, and assumptions.
ENTRY 04.01 · FREE Content

Build a 30-day authority engine

Best for: creating content that leads somewhere instead of posting randomly.

Design a 30-day content system that builds trust and leads toward one paid action.

EXPERTISE OR BRAND: [topic]
AUDIENCE: [specific audience]
PAID ACTION: [service, product, affiliate click, booking, etc.]
PLATFORMS: [list]
TIME AVAILABLE: [hours per week]
VOICE: [tone and style]
PROOF I CAN USE: [examples, results, experience, data]

Create four weekly themes and a calendar with:
- One authority post.
- One practical tutorial.
- One opinion or myth correction.
- One proof/example post.
- One conversion post.

For each piece provide the hook, core point, format, call to action, and repurposing plan. Use a 70/20/10 balance: 70% help, 20% proof or perspective, 10% direct promotion. Avoid fabricated stories or statistics.
ENTRY 07.02 · FREE Automation

Find the automations worth building

Best for: identifying high-return automation opportunities before buying software.

Act as an automation architect. Analyze this workflow and identify only automations that are worth maintaining.

BUSINESS OR TEAM: [description]
CURRENT PROCESS:
[describe each step, who does it, tools used, time spent, and frequency]

For every possible automation, score:
- Hours saved per month.
- Error reduction.
- Revenue or response-time impact.
- Setup effort.
- Monthly software cost.
- Failure and privacy risk.
- Need for human approval.

Rank the opportunities using (monthly value + risk reduction) divided by (setup effort + maintenance burden). Recommend the top three only. For each, provide a trigger-action workflow, required fields, exception handling, human checkpoint, test plan, and rollback method. Explicitly identify steps that should not be automated.
