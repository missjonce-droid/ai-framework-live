AI Framework · Accuracy Report · October 2026

AI gets AI wrong. We checked.

We asked AI questions about ten well-known AI companies — the kind of questions a customer would ask — and checked every claim against the companies' own live pages. This is what we found.

67%
of AI's claims were wrong, stale, or missing

43 claims checked. 29 wrong, stale, or missing. Average AI accuracy: 46%. Across all ten companies, scores ranged from 32% to 66% — no company scored above two-thirds. The most common error was pricing: old prices, retired plans, and renamed tiers that AI still describes as if they were current.

How we checked

For each company, we asked AI the questions customers actually ask — what it costs, what plan includes what, what the product does — then verified each claim against the company's live website and pricing page. Every claim got one of three verdicts: Correct, Stale (was true, isn't anymore), or Incorrect. Nothing was guessed. Every verdict is tied to a live source.

Why no company names? Because the point is the pattern, not embarrassing anyone. Each company already received its own full, private audit. We're in the fixing business, not the shaming business — and a business is far more likely to fix a problem it was shown privately than one it was called out for publicly.

Why this costs businesses money

When AI tells a customer the wrong price, the wrong plan, or a feature that no longer exists, that customer either walks away confused or arrives angry. The business never sees the conversation — it just loses the sale. These answers are the new storefront, and right now the storefront is wrong most of the time.

What fixes it

There is no form, badge, or payment that rewrites an AI answer. What works is fixing the sources AI reads: the company's own website, its listings, and one clean, owner-approved verified profile — then re-checking on a schedule to prove the answers moved. That is what AI Framework does: audit, correction, and monitoring, with evidence at every step.

An honest limit: no one outside the AI companies controls when their answers update. Monitoring sells evidence of movement — monthly re-checks against live sources — not a guaranteed update on a schedule.
